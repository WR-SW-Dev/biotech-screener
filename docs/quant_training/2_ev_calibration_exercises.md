# EV and Calibration Exercises for Biotech Events

### Exercise 1:
**Setup:** A mid-stage biotech company developing a novel cancer immunotherapy drug in Phase II trials. Market Cap: $500M.

**Catalyst:** Positive Phase II trial results with an ORR of 40% and a PFS of 6 months.

**EV Calculation:** 
- Current EV: $500M
- Potential Upside: $1B if the drug progresses to Phase III and achieves a successful outcome.
- Probability of Success: 30%
- Discount Rate: 20%

\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$500M + (\$1B - \$500M) \times 0.30 \times (1 / (1 + 0.20)^1) \]
\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$500M + \$400M \times 0.30 \times 0.833 \]
\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$500M + \$100M \]
\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$600M \]

**Bias:** Overconfident
- **Scenario:** Stock moves up by 20% to $600M.
- **Reasoning:** The company is overestimating the probability of success, leading to an inflated stock price.

### Exercise 2:
**Setup:** A late-stage biotech company with a promising drug in Phase III trials. Market Cap: $1B.

**Catalyst:** Positive Phase III trial results with a significant improvement in patient survival rates.

**EV Calculation:** 
- Current EV: $1B
- Potential Upside: $3B if the drug is approved and achieves widespread adoption.
- Probability of Success: 40%
- Discount Rate: 25%

\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$1B + (\$3B - \$1B) \times 0.40 \times (1 / (1 + 0.25)^1) \]
\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$1B + \$2B \times 0.40 \times 0.8 \]
\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$1B + \$640M \]
\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$1.64B \]

**Bias:** Anchored
- **Scenario:** Stock moves up by 35% to $1.72B.
- **Reasoning:** The stock price is anchored to the current market cap, ignoring the potential for significant upside if the drug succeeds.

### Exercise 3:
**Setup:** A biotech company with a novel gene therapy in early-stage trials. Market Cap: $200M.

**Catalyst:** Positive preclinical results indicating high efficacy and low toxicity.

**EV Calculation:** 
- Current EV: $200M
- Potential Upside: $5B if the drug progresses to clinical trials and achieves regulatory approval.
- Probability of Success: 10%
- Discount Rate: 30%

\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$200M + (\$5B - \$200M) \times 0.10 \times (1 / (1 + 0.30)^1) \]
\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$200M + \$4.8B \times 0.10 \times 0.769 \]
\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$200M + \$368M \]
\[ \text{Expected Value} = \$568M \]

**Bias:** Ignoring Base Rate
- **Scenario:** Stock moves up by 15% to $234M.
- **Reasoning:** The stock price is not adjusting for the low probability of success, leading to an overvalued stock.
